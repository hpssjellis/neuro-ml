🏠 Hive

Layered Brain-Flow Simulator

GitHub: hpssjellis/neuro-ml  |  LinkedIn: Jeremy Ellis
ℹ️ What this tool is (click to expand)
What this tool is: a small, fully-in-browser feedforward network visualizer that stacks arrays of "neurons" into levels, top (input) to bottom (output), and animates the signal cascading down level by level so you can watch a forward pass instead of just reading numbers. It is a loose teaching metaphor for how sensory input might flow through stacked layers of a nervous system (retina → relay → cortex-style organization, or a much simpler version of the layered wiring you'd see in something like a fruit fly's mushroom body) — not a model of any real circuit, connectome, or biological learning rule. Real brains are recurrent, sparse, and nothing like a dense matrix multiply. This is plain backpropagation on a small multilayer perceptron, dressed up so the flow of activation is visible.

ℹ️ Live Wasp Input (click to expand)
Live Wasp Input: a small striped wasp hovers above the input array and follows your mouse. Its distance from the array's center paints a proximity "hot spot" onto the input grid in real time — closer = brighter/bigger, farther = fades to nothing — instead of you clicking cells by hand. Loosely modeled on real looming-object-detector neurons in insect vision (e.g. the locust LGMD). Turn it on below, then move your mouse over the 3D view. Press the + key at any moment to capture the current input as a training example. On small grids the wasp rarely reaches the four corner cells, so the sliders below drive those corners directly with independent environmental signals — the wasp and the sliders combine into one input pattern. A small hive sits in the far corner of the scene; while Live Wasp Input is on, the "closeness to hive" corner signal is computed automatically from the bee's real distance to that hive instead of the manual slider. When "Run/Stop" is on, it samples this live input if the wasp is on, or cycles your stored examples if it's off.

ℹ️ How this works (click to expand)
Each level is a plain dense layer: every neuron in level i connects to every neuron in level i+1 with its own weight, plus a bias per neuron, squashed through a sigmoid. A forward pass computes every level instantly — the top-to-bottom "wave" you see is a deliberate playback delay added purely for visualization, not a real propagation time. Training uses standard backpropagation (mean-squared error between the bottom level's output and your desired output) across whatever example pairs you've stored. Connection lines are colored by weight sign (warm = excitatory, cool = inhibitory) and brighten as the wave reaches them. For dense layers this is capped to a random sample of the connections for rendering speed — the underlying math still uses every weight.
⚠️ Teaching-toy disclaimer (click to expand)
This is a teaching toy, not a brain model. It has no recurrence, no spiking, no biological plasticity rule, and no relationship to real cortical or connectome wiring beyond "information flows through stacked layers of units." Loosely inspired by the idea behind connectome-driven demos such as the fruit fly connectome simulation, but vastly simplified and trainable rather than fixed.
